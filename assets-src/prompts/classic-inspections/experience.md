Use case: style-transfer. Production artwork for a 1990s point-and-click adventure.
INPUT 1 is the EDIT TARGET for its concept, objects and layout. INPUT 2 is the APPROVED STYLE REFERENCE (About suitcase scene). Repaint input 1 to belong convincingly to the same game as input 2.
ART DIRECTION: Sam & Max Hit the Road and Full Throttle. The approved reference controls the degree of stylisation: strong irregular dark ink contours, deliberately stepped raster edges, compact pixel clusters, matte surfaces, 2–4 hard-edged shades per material, dramatic navy/violet shadow masses, warm ochre light, selective restrained dithering. Wry hand-drawn adventure-game personality, slightly crooked perspective and characterful graphic silhouettes. Simplify ornate detail into a few well chosen shapes. The image should read as a carefully drawn low-resolution DOS adventure screen enlarged with nearest-neighbour, not a contemporary cartoon illustration with a filter. Maintain sunny/playful appeal, avoid grim horror mood.
Keep 2:1 wide framing. Preserve the existing concept and main objects, with freedom to adjust incidental shapes to feel more authored, humorous and period appropriate. No additional characters or unrelated objects.
CRITICAL LIVE-TEXT AREA: The existing large blank panel on the RIGHT is functional space for HTML text. Retain its placement and outer extent. Keep a simple pale cream, almost uniform reading surface with no inscriptions, internal decoration, stains, shadows or objects invading the specified safe rectangle. Any thick borders, bolts or supports must stay outside that rectangle. Use crisp shapes with no artificial sharpening halos.
AVOID glossy plastic, smooth high-resolution vector curves, soft airbrush, bloom, realistic reflections, 3D lighting, sparkling lemon pores, children's mobile-game aesthetic, candy highlights, perfect symmetry, excessive miniature architectural detail, fake CRT scanlines, labels or watermarks. Do not import the man, suitcase, notebook or Sorrento scene from image 2 into this unrelated scene. This is the SAME rendering style applied to input 1's concept.
SCENE AND LAYOUT: Experience: Swiss career express. Red little locomotive with white cross, driver in blue uniform, luggage wagons with green/purple/blue cases, and a nonchalant cow lounging in the last wagon on a rising stone viaduct; Alpine mountains, evergreens and church beyond. Red-framed timetable under timber shelter at right. Blank cream reading rectangle x=54–92%, y=20–72%. Redesign the generic adorable children's-book driver and cow into eccentric 1990s adventure supporting characters with angular economical drawing and dry humour. Driver is a fictional NPC, not Daniele. Preserve the sloping train, readable cases and cow joke. Avoid shiny toy-train finish.

REVISION 2 — the customer rejected the prior Swiss people as too generic and cute. Redesign the fictional TRAIN DRIVER AND COW from scratch. Push Sam & Max Hit the Road's eccentric American comic-strip NPC visual language very clearly. Driver: a skinny, hunched middle-aged railway obsessive with a long bent nose, tiny close-set half-lidded eyes, sharply protruding chin, two stiff moustache tufts and a ludicrously tall floppy uniform cap; angular shoulders in a too-large blue jacket, bony hand gripping throttle, smug lopsided closed-mouth smirk. Absolutely no round baby face, white Mickey Mouse gloves, button nose, toddler proportions or huge friendly eyes. The cow is a lanky flat-sided deadpan passenger, not a cute mascot: long rectangular drooping muzzle, heavy lowered eyelids, tiny horn points, long elbows slung over suitcase, one hoof lazily dangling. Keep expressive uncluttered outlines and two-tone shadows. Neither character is Daniele and no likeness needs preserving here. The red locomotive is an eccentric crooked machine, leaning uphill with an overlarge smokestack and crooked cab; luggage teeters in clear graphic shapes. Mountain slopes are jagged flat-value shapes with far fewer detail strokes. Train and cow kept in left half. Really change silhouettes, staging and anatomy rather than merely pixelating old art. Keep reading rectangle at right exactly clear.
